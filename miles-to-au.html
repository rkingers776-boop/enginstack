177 Years at 60 MPH

That's how long it would take to drive one astronomical unit. 92,955,807 miles. If you left on the day the US Constitution was ratified — June 21, 1788 — and drove 60 miles per hour without ever stopping, you'd reach the Sun in early 1965. Divide miles by 92,955,807.3. The number you get will be small. Terrestrial distances are small when measured in AU. The circumference of Earth — 24,901 miles — is 0.00027 AU. The distance from New York to Los Angeles — about 2,800 miles — is 0.000030 AU. The distance from the Earth to the Moon — 238,900 miles — is 0.00257 AU. The longest drive you can take on this planet without getting on a boat — roughly 12,000 miles from Cape Town to Magadan, Russia — is 0.00013 AU. The Solar System does not care about your road trip.

The mile-to-AU conversion exists mostly as a tool for making the Solar System tangible. When Carl Sagan said "billions and billions," he was fighting the same battle every astronomy educator fights: the numbers are too big to feel. An AU is a unit you can almost hold. Picture the Sun. The Earth orbits at 1 AU. Now stack that distance: Mars at 1.5, Jupiter at 5.2, Neptune at 30. Each stack is the same gap you just pictured. The AU turns the Solar System from a list of incomprehensible mile counts into a set of proportions a child can draw with a compass and a ruler.

This page is the long way around to that perspective. Start with miles — the unit you know from highway signs and odometers. Divide by 92,955,807.3. Get a number so small it forces you to confront the actual size of things. That confrontation is the point.

The Earth-Moon distance is often used as a stepping stone to visualize the AU. At 238,900 miles, the Moon is 0.00257 AU from Earth. If the Earth were a basketball, the Moon would be a tennis ball 24 feet away. The Sun, at 1 AU, would be 93 feet across and nearly two miles down the road. The scale breaks your brain quickly. That's the intended effect.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many miles does light travel in 1 AU?

Light travels 92,955,807 miles in 499 seconds — exactly one AU. The speed of light in miles per second is about 186,282. 186,282 × 499 ≈ 92,955,807. The round number for light-time to the Sun is 8 minutes 19 seconds. The actual time varies by about ±8 seconds over the year because the orbit is elliptical.

What's the farthest human-made object in miles and AU?

Voyager 1. As of 2026, roughly 15.2 billion miles from Earth, or 163 AU. It crossed the heliopause — the boundary where the solar wind gives way to interstellar space — in August 2012 at 121 AU. It's now in interstellar space, moving at 38,000 mph relative to the Sun. At that speed, it covers about 3.6 AU per year. It will pass within 1.7 light-years of a star called AC+79 3888 in about 40,000 years. The spacecraft's plutonium power source will die around 2030. After that, it will drift silent forever — a 1,600-pound metal dot moving at 10 miles per second through a void so empty that the nearest solid object is trillions of miles away.

Can I memorize the miles-to-AU conversion factor?

You can memorize the big number: 93 million miles per AU. That gives you AU = miles ÷ 93,000,000 — an error of about 0.05%. For mental math: 100 million miles ≈ 1.075 AU. 200 million miles ≈ 2.15 AU. 500 million miles ≈ 5.38 AU (just past Jupiter). For anything requiring accuracy, use the calculator above. The exact divisor is 92,955,807.3.

Engineering Context

The Mars Climate Orbiter was lost in 1999 because one team used pound-force-seconds and another expected newton-seconds. The error — a factor of 4.45 — sent the spacecraft into the Martian atmosphere at the wrong altitude. It disintegrated. The AU-to-miles conversion carries the same hazard: if one system feeds AU into a model that expects miles, the error isn't a factor of 4.45 — it's a factor of 92,955,807. A discrepancy that large is usually caught. But the habit of checking units — of verifying that every number in the data pipeline is in the unit the consumer expects — is drilled into every aerospace engineer precisely because of errors like these. The factor doesn't matter. The habit of checking does. Length Conversion Guide.
