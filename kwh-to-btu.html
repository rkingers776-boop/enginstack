kWh to BTU

A kilowatt-hour of electricity costs money on every bill in the world, but in America it is also measured against the furnace: one kWh is exactly 3,412.14163312794 BTU, which is how heating engineers compare the price of electric heat with the price of gas. Ten kWh is 34,121 BTU — about the hourly output of a large room heater. The factor is exact because both units are exactly defined.

kWh to BTU

The kWh is exactly 3,600,000 joules; the International Steam Table BTU is exactly 1055.05585262 joules; divide and one kWh is exactly 3,412.14163312794 BTU. This is the number behind every 'electric vs gas heating' comparison, every HVAC load calculation that crosses systems, and every energy-efficiency label that quotes both BTU and kWh. Because both anchors are definitions, the factor carries full precision and never needs rounding.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many BTU are in one kWh?

Exactly 3,412.14163312794. One kWh is 3,600,000 joules; one BTU is 1055.05585262 joules (International Steam Table); the ratio is exact.

How do I compare electric and gas heating costs?

Convert both to the same unit. If electricity is $0.15/kWh and gas is $1.20/therm (100,000 BTU), then 1 kWh of electric heat costs $0.15 and delivers 3,412 BTU, while $0.15 of gas at that rate buys 12,500 BTU — gas wins on raw energy; efficiency changes the real answer.

Why does my energy bill list kWh instead of BTU?

Because the electric meter measures energy in kWh — a unit of the SI family that is convenient at the utility scale. BTU is used for HVAC equipment ratings. The two meet in efficiency calculations, which is exactly what this converter is for.
