A tire gauge reads 30 psi; a scuba table thinks in atmospheres; a European spec sheet writes bars. All three describe the same inflation, and the psi-to-atm bridge is 0.06804596390987 — the exact number of atmospheres in one pound per square inch, built from the 1959 pound, the 2.54 cm inch, and the 101,325 Pa standard atmosphere. psi to atmospheres

One psi is exactly 6894.757293168 Pa — the pound-force (0.45359237 kg × 9.80665 m/s²) over the square inch ((0.0254 m)²) — and one standard atmosphere is exactly 101,325 Pa, so one psi is exactly 101,325 / 6894.757293168 = 0.06804596390987 atm. The number carries eleven digits because it chains three treaty definitions. The practical uses: tire inflation, compressor discharge, pneumatic tools — all quoted in psi in America and atmospheres or bars abroad.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many atmospheres is 30 psi?

Exactly 2.0414 atmospheres. 30 psi × 0.06804596390987 = 2.0414 atm — the same pressure a scuba diver feels at about 34 feet of seawater.

What does psi stand for?

Pounds per square inch — more precisely, pound-force per square inch: the force exerted by one pound under standard gravity on a one-inch-square area. That definition is why psi converts to pascals through the pound and inch definitions.

How do I convert psi to atm quickly?

Divide psi by 14.696 for a good estimate — since 1 atm = 14.6959 psi, dividing by 14.7 is within 0.03%. For exact work use the full factor on this page, or divide by 14.6959487755.
