watts to amps

Every National Electrical Code load calculation works this way: sum the connected watts, divide by the circuit voltage, and the result tells you the breaker and wire size. At 120 V, 1,800 W is 15 A; at 240 V the same watts need only 7.5 A — which is why high-power appliances run on 240 V. The 80% rule (a continuous load should not exceed 80% of breaker rating) makes this conversion the first step of every circuit design.

amps = watts ÷ volts
I = P/V

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert watts to amps?

Divide watts by volts: I = P/V. A 1,800 W heater on 120 V draws 15 A. At 240 V, the same heater draws only 7.5 A.

What is the 80% rule for breakers?

The NEC says a continuous load (running 3+ hours) should not exceed 80% of the breaker rating. So a 15 A breaker handles 12 A continuous = 1,440 W at 120 V. This converter shows both the raw and the derated amps.

How many watts can a 15-amp circuit handle?

At 120 V, 1,800 W at full rating, or 1,440 W continuous per the 80% rule. Multiply the breaker amps by the circuit voltage and you have the watts — the exact inverse of this calculator.
