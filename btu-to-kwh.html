BTU to kWh

One BTU is the heat needed to raise one pound of water one degree Fahrenheit, and its exact energy equivalent is fixed by the International Steam Table: 1 BTU = 1055.05585262 joules. Divide by 3,600,000 joules per kilowatt-hour and the factor is exactly 0.0002930710702 kWh. HVAC engineers meet this at every equipment selection — converting a furnace's BTU rating to the kWh it represents, or checking an AC's cooling capacity against its electrical input to find the coefficient of performance.

kWh = BTU × 0.0002930710702

Frequently Asked Questions

How many kWh is 12,000 BTU?

Exactly 3.5169 kWh. The 12,000 BTU/hr air conditioner rating — called 'one ton' of cooling — is 12,000 × 0.0002930710702 = 3.5169 kWh of heat removed per hour.

Is BTU the same as BTU/hr?

No — they are different quantities. BTU is an amount of energy; BTU/hr is a rate of energy (power). Air conditioners are rated in BTU/hr but colloquially called '12,000 BTU units.' One BTU/hr equals 0.2930710702 watts.

What is a BTU exactly?

One BTU is the heat required to raise one pound of water by one degree Fahrenheit at standard conditions. The International Steam Table fixed its exact energy equivalent at 1055.05585262 joules, from which all modern conversions derive.
