The Half-Pound Problem

Walk through any American grocery store and the baking aisle tells the story: butter sold in 1/2 lb sticks, flour in 5 lb bags, sugar in 4 lb bags. Walk into a European kitchen and the same ingredients sit in 250 g, 500 g, and 1 kg packs. The two systems meet in every international recipe book, and the conversion is exact because the pound was defined against the kilogram in 1959: multiply by 453.59237. Half a pound of butter is 226.8 g — the number every pastry chef has memorized — and a 5 lb flour bag is 2,268 g, which is why European recipes asking for "500 g flour" look at a 5 lb US bag and take one-fifth of it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert pounds to grams?

Multiply by 453.59237: grams = pounds × 453.59237. A 1 lb block of butter is 453.6 g; half a pound is 226.8 g. The factor is exact — the 1959 agreement defines the pound as 0.45359237 kg, and a gram is one thousandth of a kilogram.

How many grams is half a pound?

226.796185 g — call it 227 g. Half a pound of butter is the single most converted US recipe amount, because American baking sticks are sold in 1/2 lb (8 oz) blocks and metric recipes call for 225–250 g. The 227 g figure is exact within half a gram.

Why do US and metric recipes use different units for the same flour?

Because volume is not weight. A US cup of flour weighs between 120 and 150 g depending on how it is scooped and sifted, which is why professional recipes — US and European alike — weigh flour in grams. The pound-to-gram conversion (1 lb = 453.59237 g) is exact; the cup-to-gram conversion is a guess until someone defines the packing method.
