mm² to AWG

European and Asian cable is sized in square millimeters — 1.5 mm² for lighting, 2.5 mm² for power circuits — and when that cable meets a US terminal, connector, or ampacity table, someone needs the AWG number: 2.5 mm² is closest to 13 AWG, 1.5 mm² to 15 AWG, 4 mm² to 11 AWG. This converter finds the exact AWG value and the nearest standard gauge.

mm² to AWG

The reverse of the AWG formula — AWG = 36 − 39·log92(d/0.127), with the diameter derived from the area as d = √(4A/π) — returns a continuous value. Since AWG only exists as integer steps, the practical result is the nearest standard gauge, which is what terminal compatibility actually needs: a 2.5 mm² conductor lands between 13 and 14 AWG, and the connector is chosen for the exact measured diameter, not the label. Imported equipment with metric terminations and US wire stock meet at this conversion daily.

Frequently Asked Questions

What AWG is 2.5 mm² cable?

2.5 mm² is closest to 13 AWG (2.62 mm² area). It is the standard European power circuit cable, typically protected at 16–20 A.

What AWG is 1.5 mm² cable?

1.5 mm² is closest to 15 AWG (1.65 mm²). It is the standard European lighting circuit cable, protected at 10–16 A.

Is the AWG match exact?

The formula gives a continuous value, but AWG only exists as integer steps — so the result is the nearest standard gauge. For terminal fit, always use the measured diameter, not the rounded gauge label.
