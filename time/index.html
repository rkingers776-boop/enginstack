Time: The One Unit System Everyone Agrees On — Until They Don't

Time conversion is deceptively simple. 60 seconds per minute, 60 minutes per hour, 24 hours per day — these ratios are universal, the only measurement system shared by every country and every culture on Earth. They trace back to the Babylonians, who used a base-60 (sexagesimal) counting system around 2000 BCE, and to the ancient Egyptians, who divided daylight into 12 hours based on sundial markings.

But time gets complicated when you zoom out. A month is not a fixed unit — it varies from 28 to 31 days. A year is 365 days most of the time, 366 in leap years — except century years not divisible by 400. A week is 7 days because of the Babylonian planetary week, which was later adopted by Jewish, Christian, and Islamic calendars. And leap seconds — added 27 times since 1972 to keep UTC aligned with Earth's slowing rotation — nearly broke the internet in 2012 when Reddit, LinkedIn, and Qantas all crashed on the same leap-second night. The leap second was officially abolished in 2035 by the General Conference on Weights and Measures.

Time Conversion Reference

1 minute = 60 seconds | 1 hour = 3,600 seconds

1 day = 86,400 seconds (except on leap-second days = 86,401 s)

1 week = 604,800 seconds | 1 month = 2,419,200 to 2,678,400 seconds

1 millisecond = 0.001 s — the unit of high-frequency trading and CPU clock cycles
