ounces to pounds — sixteen to the pound, exactly

American grocery and butcher pricing runs on two units at once: meat is cut in ounces and priced per pound. A 12 oz steak is 0.75 lb; an 8 oz stick of butter is 0.5 lb; a 16 oz porterhouse is a full pound. The conversion is the cleanest in the imperial system — divide by 16, exactly, because the avoirdupois ounce is defined as one sixteenth of a pound. The Sixteenth That Runs the Deli Counter

The imperial weight system is built on halving: a pound divides into 16 ounces, an ounce into 16 drams, and every serving size in American food retail falls out of that ladder. A quarter-pound burger is 4 oz, a half-pound cut is 8 oz, a three-quarter steak is 12 oz — the ounce exists precisely so that pound-based pricing divides cleanly. The conversion is exact because the ounce is not a separate standard: it is the pound's sixteenth part, fixed when the pound itself was defined against the kilogram in 1959. Divide by 16 and any ounce weight becomes the pound price the cashier will charge.

The 8 oz row is the one with the most second-hand mileage: an American stick of butter is exactly half a pound, which is why recipes say "one stick (8 tbsp)" and why the wrapper prints tablespoon marks on the side. Every baking conversion that starts with butter — metric recipes asking for 227 g, US recipes asking for 1/2 lb — routes through this 8 oz = 0.5 lb anchor. Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert ounces to pounds?

Divide by 16: pounds = ounces ÷ 16. A 12 oz steak is 12 ÷ 16 = 0.75 lb; 8 oz of butter is 0.5 lb. The factor is exact because the avoirdupois ounce is defined as one sixteenth of a pound.

Is a fluid ounce the same as a weight ounce?

No. The fluid ounce (fl oz) measures volume — 8 fl oz is one cup — while the ounce (oz) measures weight, 16 oz to the pound. The confusion is constant in cooking: '16 oz of water' and '16 fl oz of water' happen to weigh nearly the same because water's density is close to 1 g/mL, but 16 oz of honey (fluid) and 16 fl oz of honey weigh very differently.

Why does meat come in 8 oz, 12 oz, and 16 oz portions?

Because those are the US serving-size conventions: 8 oz is a half-pound cut, 12 oz is the classic restaurant steak, 16 oz is a full pound. Butchers price per pound, so the ounce weights exist to make the division clean — 12 oz at $14/lb is $10.50, exactly three quarters of the pound price.
