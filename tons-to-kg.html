Why Every Ton Spec Ends Up in Kilograms

Load ratings, axle limits, and structural calculations live in kilograms because the kilogram is the SI base unit and the formulas are built around it — stress in newtons per square meter needs newtons, and newtons need kilograms. The tonne is the specification's shorthand, the way a crane brochure says 100 t instead of 100,000 kg. Multiply by 1,000 and the shorthand becomes the working number. The conversion is so clean it feels trivial, which is exactly why the metric-versus-US ton error is so common: the clean ×1,000 is for metric tons, and anyone who applies it to a US short ton is off by 9.2% before the calculation even starts.

The 2.4 t row is the construction constant hiding inside the table: concrete's density of 2,400 kg/m³ is a physics property, and writing it as 2.4 t/m³ is the same number in load-spec language. A slab 5 m × 10 m × 0.2 m is 10 m³ of concrete — 24 t — and the ready-mix order, the truck count, and the foundation calculation all start from that conversion. The tonne keeps the estimate readable; the kilogram keeps the structural math honest; this page is the two-line bridge between them.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert metric tons to kilograms?

Multiply by 1,000: kilograms = tons × 1,000. A 40 t loaded semi-truck is 40 × 1,000 = 40,000 kg. The factor is exact because the tonne is defined as 1,000 kilograms.

How much does a cubic meter of concrete weigh?

About 2.4 metric tons — 2,400 kg — for standard reinforced concrete, depending on aggregate. That density is why construction estimates work in tons: a 10 m³ slab is 24 t of concrete. The same 2.4 t/m³ appears in density tables as 2,400 kg/m³, and the two are the same number written at different scales.

What is the difference between metric tons and US short tons in shipping?

A metric ton (tonne) is exactly 1,000 kg; a US short ton is 2,000 lb = 907.185 kg — 9.2% less. International shipping, rail, and construction use metric tons; US domestic road freight uses short tons. A 40 t European load limit is 88,185 lb, while the 80,000 lb US limit is 36.3 t: the same truck crosses the border and changes ton by ton system.
