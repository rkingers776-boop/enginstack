cubic meters to gallons

A pool builder working from a metric plan — 50 m³, say — and an American pool-supply catalog that sells chlorine by the gallon will meet at 264.172052358, the exact number of US gallons in a cubic meter. The number is a reciprocal of the gallon's 231 in³ definition and the 1959 inch, so it carries no approximation; it just looks alarming because it is built from an ancient English volume standard against the modern metric cube.

cubic meters to gallons

One cubic meter is exactly 264.172052358 US gallons — the factor is exact because both units are legally defined, and it is the reciprocal of 0.003785411784. The engineering situations where this matters are almost always chemical-dosing: pool chlorination, water-treatment alum feed, cooling-tower biocide, all spec the treatment in gallons while the tank volume is in cubic meters. Get the dose per m³ wrong by forgetting the 264, and the treatment is off by a factor of more than two hundred.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many gallons are in a cubic meter?

Exactly 264.172052358 US gallons. It is the reciprocal of the US gallon's volume in cubic meters: 1 gal = 231 in³ = 0.003785411784 m³, so 1 m³ = 1 / 0.003785411784 = 264.172052358 gal.

Why does pool chemistry use gallons when pool volume is metric?

Because dosing products are sold in the US market with instructions in gallons, while pool designs from metric countries quote volume in cubic meters. The mismatch is exactly why this converter exists — dose per m³ and dose per gallon are 264 apart.

How accurate is '1 m³ = 264 gallons'?

It is within 0.07% — fine for estimates. For chemical dosing, use the full factor 264.172052358, since a 0.07% error in a treatment rate can compound over large water volumes.
