watts to dBm

Converting watts to dBm is how you enter the link-budget world, where every gain and loss is added and subtracted in decibels. The formula dBm = 10·log10(P_mW) is exact, and the mental anchors are simple: every factor of 10 in watts is +10 dBm, so 1 W = 30 dBm, 10 W = 40 dBm, 0.1 W = 20 dBm. Amplifiers are rated in watts but specified in dB of gain; antennas in dBi — the whole RF chain ends up in dBm, and this converter is the entry point.

dBm = 10 × log10(watts × 1000)
1 W = 30 dBm  ·  0.1 W = 20 dBm  ·  10 W = 40 dBm
The conversion is exact — dBm is dB relative to 1 milliwatt.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many dBm is 1 watt?

Exactly 30 dBm. 1 W = 1,000 mW, and 10·log10(1000) = 30. It is the most useful anchor in the RF world.

How do I convert watts to dBm by hand?

Multiply watts by 1,000 to get milliwatts, then take 10·log10 of that. The mental shortcut: every factor of 10 in watts adds 10 dBm, so 1 W = 30, 10 W = 40, 0.1 W = 20.

Why use dBm instead of watts for radio?

Because link budgets add and subtract gains and losses, and decibels make that addition natural — a 40 dBm transmitter with a 3 dB antenna and 2 dB of cable loss ends at 41 dBm EIRP. In watts, the same calculation is an ugly multiplication.
