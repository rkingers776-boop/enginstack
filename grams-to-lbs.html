The Definition That Ended the Confusion

Before 1959, "one pound" meant different things in London, Washington, and Ottawa, each country guarding its own standard artifact. The 1959 agreement replaced all of them with a single definition: the pound is exactly 0.45359237 kilograms. That one line is why this page's conversion is exact rather than approximate — grams to pounds is not a measurement problem, it is a definition applied. Every recipe, package, and protein scoop that crosses the metric-imperial line runs through this fixed number, and the rounding to 454 g that most people carry in their heads is a convenience, not the truth.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert grams to pounds?

Divide by 453.59237 (or multiply by 0.00220462): pounds = grams ÷ 453.59237. A 500 g bag of flour is 500 ÷ 453.59237 = 1.102 lb. The factor is exact — one pound is defined as 453.59237 grams by the 1959 International Yard and Pound Agreement.

Why is 1 lb 453.59237 g and not 454?

Because 454 is an approximation and 453.59237 is the definition. In 1959 the US, UK, and Commonwealth countries fixed the pound at exactly 0.45359237 kg to end decades of drifting national standards. Rounding to 454 g is fine for cooking — the error is 0.09% — but wrong for anything where grams are weighed precisely, like mail postage or lab work.

How many grams are in a pound?

Exactly 453.59237 grams. The practical numbers: half a pound is 226.8 g, one pound is 453.6 g, and 2.2 pounds is 997.9 g — just under a kilogram. This is why the '2.2 lb per kg' rule of thumb exists: it is exact to within 0.2%.
