A power bank says 10,000 mAh. An airline says carry-on batteries stay under 100 Wh. A solar catalog sells a "500 Wh" lithium pack. These are the same quantity in different languages, and the translator is voltage: Ah = Wh ÷ V. mAh Is Marketing, Wh Is Physics

Amp-hours counts charge — how many electrons the battery can push. But electrons alone do not tell you how long a device runs, because devices consume power, and power is charge flow times voltage. A 5 Ah battery at 3.7 V holds 18.5 Wh; at 12 V it holds 60 Wh. Same amp-hour label, more than three times the energy. That is why every phone power bank advertises mAh (the bigger number) while every airline regulation, shipping label, and engineering spec uses Wh (the comparable one). When you convert Wh to Ah you are working backwards from the honest number to the marketing number — and the voltage is the key that unlocks it.

amp hours = watt hours ÷ volts
Ah = Wh / V
Exact, by the definition of the watt-hour — use the battery's nominal voltage.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert watt hours to amp hours?

Divide by the battery's voltage: amp hours (Ah) = watt hours (Wh) ÷ volts (V). A 500 Wh battery at 12 V holds 500 ÷ 12 = 41.7 Ah. The voltage is essential — the same 500 Wh at 48 V would be only 10.4 Ah, even though the energy is identical.

Why do power banks advertise mAh instead of Wh?

Because mAh is the bigger, more impressive number. A 10,000 mAh pack sounds larger than 37 Wh, but mAh without voltage is meaningless — the pack's cells run at 3.7 V, so the honest energy is 10 Ah × 3.7 V = 37 Wh. Airlines and shipping regulations use Wh for exactly this reason: it is the number that is actually comparable across different voltages.

What voltage do I use for a 12 V lead-acid or lithium battery?

Use the nominal voltage — 12 V for a '12-volt' battery, 3.7 V for a lithium-ion cell, 48 V for an e-bike pack. Nominal is the midpoint of the working range (a 12 V lead-acid actually swings 10.5–14.8 V), and it is the number battery sellers use for the rated capacity. Converting with nominal voltage gives capacity figures consistent with the datasheet.
