square feet to hectares

An American construction site is sized in square feet — 50,000 ft² for a big-box store — and the same land in a metric country's permit application reads 0.46 hectares. The factor that moves between them is 0.000009290304, small enough to look like an error and exact enough to matter: one square foot is precisely 0.000009290304 hectares, because the foot is 0.3048 m and a hectare is 10,000 m².

square feet to hectares

The square foot is a building-scale unit and the hectare a land-scale one, so their ratio is necessarily small — 1 ft² = 0.000009290304 ha exactly. The engineering content is in the decimal: developers translating a US site plan into a metric-country permit, environmental regulators converting acreage-based reports, and solar developers comparing panel areas across systems all need the full factor, not the rounded 0.0000093 that loses precision over large sites.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many hectares are in a square foot?

Exactly 0.000009290304. One square foot is (0.3048 m)² = 0.09290304 m², and a hectare is 10,000 m², so 1 ft² = 0.09290304 / 10,000 = 0.000009290304 ha.

Why do US construction sites use square feet while permits abroad use hectares?

Because the US building industry is built on the foot, while international land administration uses metric. The conversion is exact, so the real risk is decimal placement — 0.0000093 ha for one square foot is easy to misplace by a factor of ten.

How big is a hectare compared to a football field?

A hectare is 100 m × 100 m. An American football field including end zones is about 5,351 m² (57,600 ft²) — roughly 0.535 ha, so a hectare is just under two football fields.
