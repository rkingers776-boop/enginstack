torr to pascals

The torr is the vacuum scientist's unit — named for Evangelista Torricelli, who invented the mercury barometer in 1644 — and it is defined as exactly 1/760 of a standard atmosphere. Since one standard atmosphere is exactly 101,325 Pa, one torr is exactly 133.322368421053 Pa. A vacuum chamber at 1×10⁻³ torr is 0.133 Pa; a roughing pump pulling 0.1 torr is at 13.3 Pa.

torr to pascals

The torr and the pascal are both pressure units anchored to the same definition: 1 atm = 760 torr = 101,325 Pa exactly, with both relationships fixed by convention. The torr's practical home is vacuum work — sputter coating, freeze drying, semiconductor processing, all specified in torr or millitorr — while the pascal and its multiples dominate every other branch of SI pressure. Every torr value on a chamber readout has an exact pascal equivalent, and converting between them never involves a rounded constant.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a torr exactly?

A torr is exactly 1/760 of a standard atmosphere — defined so that one standard atmosphere is 760 torr. Since a standard atmosphere is exactly 101,325 Pa, one torr is exactly 101,325/760 = 133.322368421053 Pa.

Is a torr the same as a millimeter of mercury?

For practical purposes, yes — the torr was defined to equal one mmHg at standard conditions, and the two are numerically identical to the precision of any real measurement. The torr is the unit of definition; mmHg is the same value read from a mercury column.

Why does vacuum science use torr instead of pascals?

History and convenience: vacuum instruments grew out of the mercury barometer (Torricelli's invention), so the field standardized on torr and millitorr. Modern labs often quote both — a 1×10⁻³ torr process chamber is 0.133 Pa — which is why the conversion is a daily tool.
