liters per minute to liters per second

Process engineers size pumps in liters per minute; hydraulic modelers and fire codes think in liters per second. The bridge is 0.0166666666667 — one sixtieth, the exact fraction of a liter per second in a liter per minute. A 30 L/min lab pump is 0.5 L/s; a 600 L/min process line is 10 L/s. The factor is the reciprocal of 60, and it is exact even though its decimal never ends.

1 liter per minute = 0.01666667 liters per second, and 1 liter per second = 60 liters per minute. The conversion is a single fixed factor: multiply L/min by 0.01666667. Worked: 10 L/min = 0.1666667 L/s; 100 L/min = 1.666667 L/s.

liters per minute to liters per second

One L/min is 1/60 L/s = 0.0166666666667 L/s, by definition — the liter is unchanged, only the time base moves from minutes to seconds. The repeating decimal is the decimal signature of a factor built from 60, and it is exact to every digit. Laboratory dosing pumps, small process lines, and hydraulic models all convert between these two scales, and because the factor is definitional, the 0.016667 never carries measurement error — only rounding error if you cut it short.

Engineering Context

The only anchor this page needs is the time family's second — the liter is identical on both sides. The L/s to L/min page runs it backward, and the flow rate hub collects the full family. Dosing and process work also connect to the volume pages when batch totals are computed from flow over time.

When both sides are liters, this conversion is nothing but a division by sixty, and that is exactly why it is worth a dedicated page: it is the change of scale that instrument engineers live with all day. A flow transmitter is calibrated in liters per second because that is the range of the electronics and the pipe velocity; a batch log, a dosing recipe, and a control loop setpoint are all written in liters per minute because that is the range a human can hold in their head. Recording a value in the wrong one of the two is a factor-of-sixty error, which is survivable in a report and not survivable in a batch reactor.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many L/s is 1 L/min?

Exactly 0.0166666666667. It is 1/60 by definition — the same liter, one sixtieth of the flow per second.

Why does the conversion need so many digits?

Because the factor is the reciprocal of 60, which is a repeating decimal: 1/60 = 0.01666... It is exact, not approximate — you just need enough digits to avoid rounding drift in large flows.

When should I use L/s instead of L/min?

Use L/s when flows are large enough that L/min becomes a four-digit number (fire, storm, canal), and L/min for equipment-level process work. The two are the same flow at different time bases.
