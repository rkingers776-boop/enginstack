How to Convert Km to Miles

The kilometer-to-mile conversion is the reciprocal of the more famous ×1.609 multiplier. 1 km = 0.621371 miles exactly — just 1 divided by 1.609344. But the number 0.621371 doesn't stick in your head the way 1.609 does, which is why most people have a harder time with this direction. Runners use a centuries-old workaround: multiply by 5 and divide by 8. It works because 5/8 = 0.625, which is within 0.6% of 0.62137. A 10K → 10 × 5 ÷ 8 = 6.25 miles (actual: 6.214). Close enough for finish-line predictions. If you want the exact number, divide by 1.609344 on any calculator. If you want a number you can use mid-stride, 5/8 has been working since before GPS watches existed.

miles = km × 0.621371192

Frequently Asked Questions

How many miles is a 5K run?

5K = 3.10686 miles. 10K = 6.2137 miles. Half marathon (21.0975 km) = 13.1094 miles. Marathon (42.195 km) = 26.2188 miles. If you run with a GPS watch set to miles, 3.1 miles is your 5K target — when it beeps at 3.1, you're done.

What's the quickest way to convert km to miles in my head?

Multiply by 5 and divide by 8. 10 km × 5 ÷ 8 = 6.25 miles (exact: 6.214). The error is 0.6% — about 193 feet per 5K. That's less than the width of a track infield. Runners have used this shortcut for decades because it works mid-stride when you can't pull out a phone.

What was the Gimli Glider incident?

On July 23, 1983, Air Canada Flight 143 ran out of fuel at 41,000 feet because the ground crew miscalculated the fuel load — they used pounds when the new Boeing 767's instruments expected kilograms. The plane glided 100 miles to an emergency landing at a decommissioned RCAF base in Gimli, Manitoba. Zero fatalities. It was Canada's metrication in action: km on the roads, kg in the cockpit — and one confused conversion that became the textbook case study for why unit labels matter.

Do GPS devices use km or miles internally?

Kilometers — always. GPS uses the WGS 84 reference ellipsoid, which is defined in meters. Every latitude/longitude coordinate is converted to meters for distance calculations. Your navigation app computes the route in metric and converts to miles at the display layer, so every US driver is running km-to-miles conversion silently, millions of times per second. It may be the most frequently executed unit conversion in computing.

Engineering Context

The km-to-miles conversion is silently embedded in every GPS chip on the planet. The WGS 84 datum defines the Earth's shape in meters — every lat/lon pair resolves to a meter-scale coordinate before any distance is computed. Turn-by-turn navigation, geofencing, fleet telematics, even the ETA on your rideshare app: all computed in meters, displayed in whatever unit the local market expects. The conversion is lossless because the source data is metric and the display conversion is multiplication by a fixed constant. But fleet operators who store trip distances in miles and then convert back to km for maintenance intervals (which are specified in km by European and Japanese manufacturers) risk double-conversion drift. The best practice, adopted by every major logistics platform, is: store everything in meters, convert only at the UI.
