atmospheres to psi

The atmosphere is one of the oldest pressure units — defined by the weight of the Earth's air at sea level and fixed at exactly 101,325 Pa — while the psi is the American industrial unit built from the pound and inch definitions. Their ratio is exact: 101,325 / 6894.757293168 = 14.69594877551. Pressure-vessel ratings, diving decompression tables, and pneumatic specs all cross this line when an international standard meets a US gauge.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many psi is one atmosphere?

Exactly 14.69594877551 psi. One standard atmosphere is 101,325 Pa, and one psi is 6894.757293168 Pa, so the ratio is exact.

Why do scuba divers use atmospheres instead of psi?

Because depth pressure in atmospheres maps directly to decompression physiology — each 33 ft of seawater adds one atmosphere. The tank gauge still reads psi in US equipment, so divers routinely convert between the two.

What is 'one atmosphere' of pressure?

It is the average pressure exerted by Earth's atmosphere at sea level — defined as exactly 101,325 Pa (14.6959 psi, 760 torr, 1.01325 bar). It is the reference point for gauge pressure, which reads zero at one atmosphere.
