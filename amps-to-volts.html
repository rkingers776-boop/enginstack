amps to volts

Ohm's law works in any direction: V = I·R, or with power, V = P/I. The reverse direction is how a charger works — a phone charging at 2 A through an effective load needs the voltage its circuitry demands. Automotive 12 V systems, USB 5 V, and the 48 V of telecom racks are all answers to the same question: what voltage do these amps need?

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert amps to volts?

Multiply amps by resistance: V = I·R. Or divide power by current: V = P/I. A 15 A current through an 8 Ω load requires 120 V.

Why do phone chargers output 5 volts?

Because the USB standard chose 5 V as its baseline. The charger's circuitry converts mains voltage down to the level the device needs — and the device draws whatever current its load requires at that voltage.

Can I calculate volts without resistance?

Yes — if you know power: V = P/I. A 60 W device drawing 5 A needs 12 V. Either resistance or power completes the pair; volts alone are never computable from amps alone.
