The 3.28 That Shows Up Everywhere

There is only one number hiding behind this page: 3.28084, the number of feet in a meter. It is the same constant that powers meters to feet for length, and because velocity inherits its length factor, it powers m/s-to-ft/s for speed. Learn it once and the whole chain opens: 1 m/s is 3.28 ft/s, 10 m/s is 32.8 ft/s, 26.82 m/s is the famous 88 ft/s of 60 mph. Every time an SI velocity needs to be spoken in the imperial world — a European wind forecast, a metric flow rate, a CFD output — this single multiplier is the translation.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert m/s to ft/s?

Multiply by 3.28084: ft/s = m/s × 3.28084. A 10 m/s wind is 10 × 3.28084 = 32.8 ft/s. The factor is the exact reciprocal of 0.3048, since 1 ft = 0.3048 m by the 1959 definition.

What is the wind speed on the Beaufort scale in m/s and ft/s?

A fresh breeze (Beaufort 5) is 8.0–10.7 m/s, which is 26.2–35.1 ft/s; a gale (Beaufort 8) is 17.2–20.7 m/s, or 56.4–67.9 ft/s. Weather services publish m/s or km/h, but US construction safety specs — crane operation, scaffolding — still quote wind limits in ft/s or mph, so the same storm crosses both units.

Why does 3.28 appear in so many conversions?

Because 3.28084 is meters-per-second's footprint in feet — the reciprocal of the 0.3048 m foot. The same number appears in meters-to-feet length conversion (meters to feet) and in m/s-to-ft/s speed conversion, because velocity inherits the length factor. If you know 1 m = 3.28 ft, you know the whole speed chain.
