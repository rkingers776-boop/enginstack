Sixteen, Because Halving Is How Food Is Sold

The metric system chose 10 because it is decimal. The imperial system chose 16 because it halves: a pound divides into two half-pounds, four quarter-pounds, eight 2-oz servings, sixteen 1-oz servings — and every one of those divisions is a whole number. That is not an accident; it matches the reality of how food is cut and portioned. A butcher's quarter-pound patty, a deli's quarter-pound slice batch, a cheese block scored into 1-oz servings — all of them are clean under 16. Multiply a pound by 16 and the entire US serving-size ladder falls out.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert pounds to ounces?

Multiply by 16: ounces = pounds × 16. Half a pound of butter is 0.5 × 16 = 8 oz; a 2 lb block of cheese is 32 oz. The factor is exact because the avoirdupois ounce is one sixteenth of the pound.

Why is a pound divided into 16 ounces and not 10?

Because 16 divides evenly by 2, 4, and 8 — a pound can be halved four times and stay whole (8, 4, 2, 1 oz). That halving convenience is why the imperial system kept 16: it matches how food is actually sold, cut, and portioned. The metric gram, by contrast, is decimal because it divides by 10 and 100.

How many ounces are in a pound of butter?

16 oz — and an American stick of butter is half that, 8 oz, which is why recipes call for 'one stick' instead of 'half a pound'. A pound block of butter, sold in some markets, is exactly the full 16 oz. In grams that same pound is 453.6 g, so a 16 oz block and a 454 g block are the same butter in two label systems.
