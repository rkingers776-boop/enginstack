cubic feet to liters

The 28.316846592 liters in a cubic foot is one of the cleanest constants on this site — it is not measured, it is derived from a treaty definition. The practical range is wide: a 10 ft³ aquarium is 283 L; a 100 ft³ workshop air volume is 2,832 L; a standard 20 lb propane cylinder holds about 7.5 ft³ of liquid, roughly 212 L. Every one of these numbers travels through the same factor, which is why a converter beats mental arithmetic — nobody has 28.316846592 memorized.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many liters are in a cubic foot?

Exactly 28.316846592. This comes from the 1959 agreement defining 1 ft = 0.3048 m exactly: a cubic foot is (0.3048)³ = 0.028316846592 m³, which is 28.316846592 liters since 1 m³ = 1,000 L.

Why is the liter-to-cubic-foot factor so ugly?

Because the two units come from different worlds: the liter is a decimal fraction of the meter, while the cubic foot is the cube of a treaty-defined foot. The cube of 0.3048 has ten significant digits, and the conversion inherits all of them.

What does a cubic foot of water weigh?

About 62.428 lb (28.316846592 kg). The famous 62.4 lb/ft³ figure that appears in every American hydraulics textbook is this number rounded — the density of fresh water at room temperature.
