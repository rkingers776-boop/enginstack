acres to square meters

The acre's metric value is one of the most useful exact constants in real-estate work, because every American deed eventually meets a metric survey. One acre = 4,046.8564224 m² exactly — no rounding, because the 1959 treaty fixed both the yard and the acre simultaneously. Land surveyors working across systems multiply acres by 4,046.8564224 and get square meters to full precision; dividing by the same constant does the reverse. The number also maps neatly to the hectare: 2.4710538 acres make a hectare.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many square meters is one acre?

Exactly 4,046.8564224 m², per the 1959 yard-and-pound agreement. This is a legal definition, not a rounded figure — the acre's metric value is exact to ten digits.

How many acres are in a hectare?

Exactly 2.4710538146717. A hectare is 10,000 m², and since an acre is 4,046.8564224 m², the ratio is 10,000 / 4,046.8564224.

Why do American deeds still use acres?

Because the US land-survey system — the Public Land Survey System of townships and sections — was built on the acre and the mile in the 18th century, and every parcel boundary since has been recorded in those units. The acre persists by legal continuity, not by preference.
