How to Convert Meters to Feet

One meter equals 3.280839895 feet exactly — the mathematical inverse of the 1959 definition that fixed 1 foot = 0.3048 meters. That's roughly 3 feet and 3 3/8 inches. The meter was originally defined in 1791 as one ten-millionth of the distance from the equator to the North Pole along the Paris meridian. Since 1983, it's been defined by the distance light travels in 1/299,792,458 of a second. The foot, once based on the length of a human foot (seriously — that varied by city), is now legally defined entirely in terms of the meter. So every time you convert meters to feet, you're using the meter to define the foot, not the other way around. The multiplier is 3.28084; the precision is infinite.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many feet are in a meter exactly?

1 meter = 3.280839895 feet exactly. Since 1 foot is legally defined as 0.3048 meters (1959), the inverse is 1/0.3048 = 3.280839895... The decimal repeats, but the relationship is exact — there is no rounding in the definition. For construction: 1 m ≈ 3' 3 3/8\". For real estate: 1 m ≈ 3.28 ft. For aerospace: carry all eight decimals.

How do I visualize a meter in everyday terms?

A meter is about the height of a doorknob from the floor, or one long adult stride. A 2-meter ceiling is 6'7\". A standard US 8-foot ceiling is 2.44 meters. An Olympic swimming pool is 50 meters = 164 feet. A 100-meter dash is 328 feet — football field plus both end zones.

Why do architects care so much about meters-to-feet conversion?

International architecture projects routinely mix metric and imperial teams. When a structural engineer in Stuttgart designs a 6-meter column grid and the contractor in Chicago builds to feet, 6 m = 19.685 ft. Round that to 19.7 or 20 feet and the columns shift by up to 3.8 inches — enough to fail a field inspection and trigger a change order worth six figures. BIM software (Revit, ArchiCAD) carries full internal precision — the rule is: never round intermediate coordinates, only round annotated dimensions on the printed sheet.

How do I convert meters to feet and inches?

Multiply by 3.28084 to get decimal feet. The whole number is your feet. Multiply the decimal remainder by 12 for inches. Example: 1.75 m → 1.75 × 3.28084 = 5.74147 ft → 5 ft + (0.74147 × 12) = 5' 8.9\". Quick reference for human heights: 1.60 m = 5'3\", 1.70 m = 5'7\", 1.80 m = 5'11\", 1.90 m = 6'3\", 2.00 m = 6'7\".

Engineering Context

Aviation uses feet for altitude worldwide — a rare case where an imperial unit is the global standard. Flight levels are hundreds of feet: FL350 means 35,000 feet. Meanwhile, terrain databases, satellite altimetry, and every national mapping agency report elevations in meters. Every terrain awareness and warning system (TAWS) on a commercial aircraft performs real-time meters-to-feet conversion against a digital elevation model, comparing the aircraft's barometric altitude (in feet) against the terrain database (in meters). A conversion lag or rounding error at this interface, in mountainous terrain with 500-foot-per-minute climb rates, is measured in seconds to ground impact. Since the 1995 American Airlines Flight 965 CFIT accident in Colombia — where confusion between meters and feet in the approach chart contributed to the crash — the ICAO standard requires terrain databases to carry both metric and imperial elevations, and TAWS algorithms must cross-check both numbers independently.
