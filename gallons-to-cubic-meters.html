gallons to cubic meters

The US gallon is defined as exactly 231 cubic inches — a definition older than the metric system itself — which means it converts to cubic meters through a chain of exact constants: 231 in³, the 1959 inch of 2.54 cm, and the liter's cubic-decimeter definition. An 8,500-gallon fire truck is 32.18 m³ of water; a 500-gallon fuel tank is 1.89 m³. Every one of those numbers is exact, never rounded, because every step in the chain is a definition.

gallons to cubic meters

The gallon's 231 in³ definition dates to the English wine gallon and was fixed by the US in the 19th century; the 1959 agreement then pinned the inch to 2.54 cm exactly. Multiply the chain — 231 × (0.0254)³ — and one US gallon is exactly 0.003785411784 m³. International projects meet this factor at every fuel depot and water tank where an American spec meets a metric one. Note this is the US gallon; the imperial gallon (4.546 L) is a different animal covered elsewhere.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many US gallons are in a cubic meter?

Exactly 264.172052358. The US gallon is 231 in³ = 231 × (0.0254)³ m³ = 0.003785411784 m³, so one cubic meter is the reciprocal: 1 / 0.003785411784 = 264.172052358 gallons.

Why is the US gallon 231 cubic inches?

It descends from the English wine gallon of the 18th century, which was standardized at 231 in³. The US kept that definition when it fixed its units in the 19th century, and it survives today as a legal definition — the British imperial gallon (277.42 in³) went a different way.

Is the imperial gallon the same as the US gallon?

No. The imperial gallon is exactly 4.54609 L, about 20% larger than the US gallon (3.785411784 L). The two share a name and a history but not a volume — always confirm which gallon a spec uses.
