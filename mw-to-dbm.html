milliwatts to dBm

dBm = 10·log10(P_mW) is exact, and the conversion matters because equipment picks one unit per context: sensor data sheets quote mW, radio engineers quote dBm, and optical power meters often display both. The practical band for receivers is picowatts (−90 dBm) up through milliwatts — this calculator covers the mW side where the numbers stay readable, and it is the bridge between the lab bench and the link budget.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many dBm is 1 milliwatt?

Exactly 0 dBm — the definition of the scale. 10 mW is 10 dBm, 100 mW is 20 dBm, and 1 W (1,000 mW) is 30 dBm.

Why does receiver sensitivity use negative dBm?

Because received signals are tiny — a good receiver hears down to about −90 dBm, which is one picowatt. The dB scale keeps such extreme smallness readable: −90 dBm beats writing 0.000000001 W.

How do I convert mW to dBm by hand?

Count powers of 10: 1 mW = 0 dBm, 10 mW = 10 dBm, 100 mW = 20 dBm. For other values use 10·log10(mW) exactly, or the reverse of 10^(dBm/10).
