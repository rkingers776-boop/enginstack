Ton to BTU/h — reading the contractor's quote

A contractor quotes a "3-ton" system, and the number means nothing until you translate it into the language the equipment actually speaks. The conversion is one clean multiplication: 1 ton of refrigeration is 12,000 BTU/h, so a 3-ton unit is 36,000 BTU/h of cooling. That is the entire trick — and once you know it, every quote, every spec sheet, and every sizing conversation snaps into focus.

The Twelve-Thousand Rule

Every ton-to-BTU conversion in HVAC reduces to multiplying by twelve thousand, and there is nothing approximate about it — the ton of refrigeration is defined as 12,000 BTU/h. The origin is the ice trade: one short ton of ice, 2,000 pounds, absorbs 144 BTU per pound as it melts, for 288,000 BTU total, and spread over a day that is 12,000 BTU per hour. When mechanical refrigeration replaced harvested ice, the trade kept the ton so customers could size a compressor against the icehouse they already knew. A century later the arithmetic is still just "tons times twelve thousand."

The square-footage column is a shortcut, not a sizing method. Two houses with identical floor plans can need different tonnage if one faces west with single-pane windows and the other is shaded with modern insulation. The only honest way to size is a Manual J load calculation; the ton rating is its answer, rounded to the nearest half-ton, and then you multiply by twelve thousand to see it in BTU/h.

Engineering Context

The reverse direction is BTU/h to ton. To see a ton rating in SI power, run it through BTU/h to kW — a 3-ton system is 10.55 kW of cooling — or read it in BTU/h to watts. For the energy side, BTU to kWh puts the nameplate in electricity-bill terms, and kWh to BTU runs the other way.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many BTU is a 3-ton air conditioner?

36,000 BTU/h. One ton of refrigeration is 12,000 BTU/h, so a 3-ton system removes heat at 3 × 12,000 = 36,000 BTU per hour. A 2.5-ton unit is 30,000 BTU/h and a 5-ton unit is 60,000 BTU/h — just multiply the tons by twelve thousand.

What does '3-ton AC' actually mean?

It means the unit removes heat at the same rate that melting three tons of ice per day would — 36,000 BTU per hour. It is not a weight and it is not the machine's physical size. The 'ton' is a unit of cooling power inherited from the ice trade, before mechanical refrigeration existed.

How do I know how many tons of AC I need?

The ton rating comes from a load calculation (Manual J in North America) that accounts for square footage, climate, insulation, window area, and ceiling height. A rough rule of thumb is about one ton per 500 to 600 square feet, but that can be off by half a ton in either direction — undersized units run constantly, oversized units short-cycle and leave humidity behind.
