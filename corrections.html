This page records every correction made to EnginStack: what changed, when, and why. It exists because a reference site is only as trustworthy as its willingness to say where it was wrong.

Correction Policy

  • Corrections are logged whether or not a reader reported them. A log that contains only reader complaints measures the readers, not the site.
  • A correction names its source. If a constant changes, the standard that governs it is named. If a historical claim changes, the investigation record is named.
  • Disagreement is stated, not resolved by vote. Where two authorities give different figures, the page gives both and the range, rather than choosing the version that is repeated most often.
  • Silent edits are not made to conversion values. The constants themselves are exact and should not need editing; when one does, it means a page was wrong, and being wrong is something this log exists to record.

How to Report a Correction

Email support@enginstack.com with five things: the page URL, the value or sentence you believe is wrong, the value you believe is correct, the source you are checking it against, and whether the error changes any answer the page gives. Reports that include a citation are verified first because the work is then confirmation rather than research.

If you are checking a conversion constant, the most useful sources are listed in full on the methodology page.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I report an error on EnginStack?

Email support@enginstack.com with the page URL, the value or statement you believe is wrong, the value you believe is correct, and the source you are checking it against. A report that names the standard is acted on fastest, because the fix is then a matter of confirming the citation rather than re-deriving the constant from scratch.

Do you publish corrections even when nobody noticed the error?

Yes. The log below includes changes that no reader reported. A reference site that only records the corrections someone complained about is telling you about its readers, not about its accuracy. Silent fixes destroy the only evidence a reader has that the site is being maintained.

What happens if a published standard changes and a page becomes outdated?

The page is updated and the change is logged with the date the standard changed and the date this site followed it. Recent examples include the retirement of the US survey foot at the end of 2022 and the 2019 redefinition of the SI base units.

Do you remove pages instead of correcting them?

Not as a way of hiding an error. A page is only withdrawn if the subject itself no longer exists, and if that happens the withdrawal is recorded in the log. Quietly deleting a page that contained a mistake would leave every inbound link and every reader who copied the number still holding the bad value.

Can I reuse a conversion from this site?

The constants themselves are legal definitions or published conversion factors and belong to nobody. The wording, the layout and the case-study research are this site's work, and republication requires an attribution link to the original page. Each page carries that condition in its metadata.
