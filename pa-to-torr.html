pascals to torr

The relationship is exact because both units are anchored to the same definition — 1 atm = 101,325 Pa = 760 torr — so one pascal is exactly 760/101,325 = 0.0075006168270417 torr. The direction matters in practice: process specs written in pascals (the SI-standard way for international equipment) meet chamber controllers and gauge readouts still calibrated in torr and millitorr, and the conversion is the daily translation between the two dialects of vacuum engineering.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many torr are in one pascal?

Exactly 0.0075006168270417. Since 1 atm = 101,325 Pa = 760 torr by definition, one pascal is 760/101,325 torr.

Why do vacuum specs switch between pascals and torr?

International equipment vendors write process specs in SI (pascals); legacy US chamber hardware and gauges read in torr/millitorr. The same chamber often displays one and the manual specifies the other — hence the daily conversion.

What pressure is high vacuum in torr?

High vacuum is conventionally 1×10⁻³ to 1×10⁻⁷ torr — about 0.133 to 1.33×10⁻⁵ Pa. Ultra-high vacuum starts at 1×10⁻⁷ torr. Converting the two scales is exactly what this page does.
