US gallons per minute to cubic feet per minute

HVAC engineers routinely compare a cooling tower's water flow in gallons per minute with its air flow in cubic feet per minute — two fluids, two flow units, one chiller. The bridge is 0.1336805556, the exact CFM in one GPM: the 231 in³ gallon divided by the 1,728 in³ cubic foot. A 60 GPM condenser water loop is 8.02 CFM of water, against which the tower might move 10,000 CFM of air.

US gallons per minute to cubic feet per minute

The factor is exact because both units are inch-derived volumes: one gallon is 231 in³ and one cubic foot is 1,728 in³, so one GPM is 231/1,728 = 0.1336805556 CFM. This is the only non-decimal factor in the flow family on this site — the rest chain through the liter — and it is the one HVAC technicians hit when balancing water-side and air-side flows. The reciprocal is 7.4805194805, the famous gallons-in-a-cubic-foot number that appears in every tank and pool calculation.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many CFM is 1 GPM?

Exactly 0.1336805556. One US gallon is 231 in³ and one cubic foot is 1,728 in³, so the ratio is 231/1,728 — exact by definition.

Why compare water GPM with air CFM?

Because cooling towers and chillers balance two fluids: the water loop carries heat away from the building, and the air stream carries it to the atmosphere. Sizing both sides of that balance means converting between the two flow units.

Is 7.48 gallons per cubic foot the same factor?

Yes — 1 ft³ = 7.4805194805 US gallons is the exact reciprocal of the 0.13368 here. It is the same ratio seen from the other side, and it appears in every tank sizing and pool volume calculation.
